About the author

Rachel Yanyi Zhang

I grew up moving between Finnish, Chinese, and English speaking environments. Each move made me more attentive to the small social rules carried by everyday language.

Why I built this

I was born in Finland and moved to China when I was ten. English later became the main language of my education. Across these environments, I began noticing moments that vocabulary lists rarely explain. The same joke could feel warm with one friend and awkward with another. A familiar expression could also change once the speaker or setting changed.

Between the Lines grew from those observations. I wanted to trace the social knowledge carried by slang and colloquial speech. The atlas looks at who can comfortably use an expression and how its meaning shifts across relationships.

The final chapter turns toward multilingual experience. It explores instinctive language choice and the feeling of moving between different social registers. These moments have shaped how I understand fluency as a form of social attention.

What I hope a visitor takes away I hope the atlas makes the hidden social layer of language easier to notice. For someone learning a language or entering an unfamiliar community, understanding that layer can make communication feel more precise and more human.
