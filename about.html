About the author

Rachel Yanyi Zhang

I grew up moving between Finnish, Chinese, and English speaking environments. Over time, I became less interested in whether a sentence was grammatically correct and more interested in why the same sentence could feel warm in one setting and strange in another.

Why I built this

I was born in Finland and moved to China when I was ten. English later became the main language of my education. Moving between these linguistic environments made me notice small social adjustments that rarely appear in vocabulary lists. A joke could require a different level of closeness. A compliment could invite acceptance in one conversation and modest rejection in another. A slang word could be completely understandable while still feeling impossible for the wrong speaker to use naturally.

Between the Lines began from that gap. I wanted to document not only what expressions mean, but what speakers need to know before those expressions work. The project therefore treats slang and colloquial language as evidence of social expectations. It asks who can say a phrase, what relationship makes it natural, and what changes when the same social task moves into another language.

The final section of the atlas turns that method toward multilingual experience itself. It looks at moments when language choice becomes part of identity and instinct. These include the language that appears under pressure, the slang a speaker understands but does not feel entitled to use, and the mixed sentence that can feel more precise than any one-language version.

What I hope a visitor takes away This is not a guide for performing another culture perfectly. It is an invitation to notice the layer of communication that sits between dictionary meaning and social meaning. For language learners, multilingual speakers, and anyone entering an unfamiliar community, that layer can determine whether a sentence merely makes sense or actually belongs in the conversation.
