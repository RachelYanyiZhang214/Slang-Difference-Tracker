An interactive atlas of cultural pragmatics

Between
the Lines

Between the Lines follows fifteen ordinary social situations across eight linguistic and cultural contexts. Receiving praise, asking for help, disagreeing, making small talk, and the other cases each reveal a different expectation about how people manage relationships through language. Every situation includes three socially workable responses that show a range within each context. It also includes a Cultural Boundary that exposes a local boundary and an Always Inappropriate response that helps identify behavior that is broadly unacceptable.

How the project works
Same situation.
Different expectations.

The fifteen situations create 120 cross-cultural cases. The comparison begins with a shared social moment and asks what an appropriate response can look like in each context. The three scenarios show variation within a culture. The Conversation Lab shows what happens when a response approaches or crosses a local expectation. The Always Inappropriate response provides a control across all eight contexts.

The Parallel Atlas

Each row begins with one of the fifteen shared social situations. Move horizontally to see how the same moment is handled across eight linguistic and cultural contexts. The comparison follows culturally characteristic strategies instead of searching for direct equivalents. Open a card to see how the response changes across three scenarios and where the local boundary becomes visible.

HOW TO READ A CASEThe large response shows the first scenario in that situation. The two smaller responses show how expectations shift when the relationship, setting, or social pressure changes. The Cultural Boundary is understandable language that crosses a culturally meaningful boundary. The Always Inappropriate response gives a shared point of comparison because it is unacceptable across the eight contexts.
Interactive social boundary test

Conversation Lab

Choose one of the fifteen situations and a cultural context. The Lab turns the selected response into a short exchange so you can see what happens before the phrase appears and how the other person is likely to receive it. Switch between the three workable scenarios, the Cultural Boundary, and the shared control to watch the interaction change.

Project architecture

What the fifteen situations reveal

01 · RESPONSE RANGE

Each situation contains three plausible scenarios. Together they show how a response can change with familiarity, setting, hierarchy, or emotional stakes while remaining socially workable.

02 · CULTURAL BOUNDARY

This response is understandable and grammatically possible. Its mismatch reveals a local expectation about modesty, directness, privacy, obligation, disagreement, or another social boundary tested by the situation.

03 · SHARED CONTROL

The final response is inappropriate across all eight contexts. It helps distinguish a culturally specific mismatch from behavior that damages the interaction more generally.
