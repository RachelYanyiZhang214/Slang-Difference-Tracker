How to read this project

Slang is evidence, not a census. An expression can reveal a recurring communicative norm without describing every speaker or every situation.

01 / PRAGMATICS

Translate the social job

Ask what the utterance is doing: opening contact, softening a request, signaling solidarity, showing care, lowering distance, or marking hierarchy.

02 / CONTRAST

Find functional equivalents

Compare expressions that do similar interpersonal work, even when their literal wording is completely different.

03 / CAUTION

Separate pattern from stereotype

A phrase may index a value in a context. It does not prove that all members of a culture share one personality or worldview.

The most interesting translation error is not when a word is wrong. It is when every word is right — and the relationship is wrong.

BETWEEN THE LINES

A personal digital humanities project on slang, cultural pragmatics, and third-culture observation. Designed as a living archive: each phrase can be revised, sourced, and expanded as new speakers contribute.

Research note

Entries distinguish attested pragmatic functions from interpretive cultural commentary. Sources include sociolinguistic research and reputable cultural references. The project intentionally avoids treating national cultures as homogeneous.
