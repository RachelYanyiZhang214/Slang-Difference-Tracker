Between the Lines is an interactive atlas of slang, greetings, and everyday expressions across languages. Instead of simply translating words, it compares how different cultures handle the same social situations — checking in, encouraging someone, easing awkwardness, recognizing effort, and reacting to something surprising. Explore the Parallel Atlas for side-by-side comparisons, click any expression for deeper cultural and pragmatic context, or use the Conversation Simulator to see how the phrase actually works inside a realistic exchange with English translations.

How to explore
Compare it. Open it. Put it into conversation.

Start with the Parallel Atlas to compare how eight linguistic contexts respond to the same five social situations. Click any expression to uncover its literal meaning, social function, cultural interpretation, and important limitations. Then send that phrase directly into the Conversation Simulator to see who might say it, when they might use it, how another person could respond, and what the entire exchange means in English.

The Parallel Atlas

The Parallel Atlas compares how the same social situation is expressed across eight linguistic contexts. Read across each category to compare the phrases side by side. Click any language card to open additional information about its literal wording, social function, cultural implications, and limits of interpretation. From that expanded card, you can also send the expression directly to the Conversation Simulator and see it used inside a short dialogue with English translations.

HOW TO EXPLORE THIS ATLAS ↓ Each row begins with one social situation. Scroll horizontally to compare how all eight linguistic contexts respond to that same moment. Each card first gives you the expression, its literal wording, and its social function. Click any card to uncover a deeper explanation, including what makes the expression interesting comparatively and what should not be overgeneralized. At the bottom of the expanded card, select “Open this phrase in the Conversation Simulator” to watch the expression operate inside a realistic exchange with line-by-line English translation.

Conversation Simulator

A phrase becomes much easier to understand once you can see who says it, what happened immediately before it, and how the other person responds. The simulator places expressions from the atlas into short social situations so you can see the difference between dictionary meaning and meaning in use.

HOW TO USE THIS ↓ Pick a social situation, then choose a language. Press Simulate conversation to generate an example exchange. The larger heading shows the expression being explored, while each dialogue line appears first in the original language and then in English directly underneath. The examples show one plausible use of each expression — they are not fixed scripts or the only situations in which the phrase can appear.

Why this matters: literal definitions often tell you almost nothing about timing, relationship, tone, or expected response. The simulator restores that missing pragmatic context by showing the expression inside an interaction.

How to interpret the project

These are pragmatic comparisons, not national personality tests. A phrase can reveal a recurring interactional pattern without describing every speaker.

01 / CONTROL

Same situation

Every language answers the same interactional question. This prevents the atlas from becoming a random collection of “interesting foreign words.”

02 / CONTEXT

Function before translation

The social job comes first: greet, encourage, reassure, recognize effort, react. Literal wording comes second.

03 / CAUTION

Pattern, not stereotype

Region, age, relationship, tone and setting all change language. Each card therefore includes a “do not overgeneralize” note.
